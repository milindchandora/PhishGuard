About this project

PhishGuard is a machine-learning phishing website detector built as a final-year college major project. It demonstrates a complete applied ML workflow: dataset preparation, feature engineering, model training and evaluation, and deployment behind a web interface and API.

What counts as phishing here

Phishing sites imitate a trusted brand's login or payment page to steal credentials or payment details. Because attackers rarely control the real domain, the URL itself tends to carry tells: extra subdomains, brand names glued onto unrelated domains, IP addresses in place of a hostname, and urgency words like "verify" or "suspended".

Why URL-only features

Analyzing only the URL — rather than fetching the page's content — keeps the check fast, avoids visiting potentially dangerous pages, and works even when a site is offline or geofenced. The trade-off is that some phishing pages hosted on otherwise clean domains, or legitimate pages with unusual URLs, can be misclassified. Treat every prediction as a strong signal, not a certainty.

Model

Several classifiers — logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, k-nearest neighbors, SVM, and naive Bayes — are trained on the same feature set and compared on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The best performer by F1 score is saved and used for live predictions. Full metrics are written to model/evaluation_report.json after training.

Limitations

  • Trained on a fixed dataset; it will not catch novel phishing patterns it has never seen.
  • Looks only at the URL, not the live page content or certificate details.
  • A "Safe" result means the URL looks structurally normal, not that the destination is verified trustworthy.
